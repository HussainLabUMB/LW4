The people behind the science

A small, interdisciplinary lab drawn from neuroscience, physiology and engineering — led by Dr. Rashad Hussain in the Department of Neurosurgery and UM-MIND.

Principal Investigator

Rashad Hussain, PhD

Associate Professor of Neurosurgery

Medicine Institute for Neuroscience Discovery (UM-MIND)
University of Maryland School of Medicine

Dr. Hussain’s work sits at the junction of fundamental and translational neuroscience: glial biology, white matter pathology, traumatic brain injury, sleep regulation, and dysfunction of the glymphatic system. Early in his career he established that stimulating neural androgen receptors can drive myelin repair in chronic demyelination — work published in Brain and protected by two patents on myelin regeneration.

His current programme asks a deceptively simple question: what happens to the brain’s fluid transport when it is injured, and can restoring that flow change outcomes? The answer, reported in Nature in 2023, was that the noradrenaline surge following trauma shuts down glymphatic and cervical lymphatic drainage — and that blocking adrenergic signalling reopens it, resolving oedema and improving recovery.

Education & training

The path here

From reproductive physiology in Pakistan to myelin biology in France and the United States, Dr. Hussain’s training spans four countries and three continents of neuroscience.

  1. 2013–2017 Postdoctoral fellow · Cell & Developmental Biology University of Colorado, Denver
  2. 2012–2013 Postdoctoral fellow · Neural Sciences Temple University, Philadelphia
  3. 2011–2012 Postdoctoral fellow · Biopathologie de la Myéline, Neuroprotection Université de Strasbourg, France
  4. 2011 PhD, Neurosciences · Signalling & Integrated Networks Université Paris-Saclay, France
  5. 2003 MSc, Zoology · Reproductive Physiology PMAS Arid Agriculture University, Rawalpindi

The team

Who works here

The lab brings together postdoctoral fellows, graduate students, research staff and undergraduates. Members learn survival microsurgery, cranial-window preparation, two-photon and mini-2P imaging, CSF tracer kinetics, particle-tracking velocimetry, immunohistochemistry and behavioural assessment — often all on the same project.
