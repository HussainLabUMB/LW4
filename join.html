We are recruiting curious, rigorous scientists to study how the brain clears its own waste — and how to fix that machinery after injury and environmental exposure. Positions are funded by the NIH and the U.S. Department of Defense.

Open position

Postdoctoral Fellow — Glymphatic Neuroscience & Neurotrauma

Department of Neurosurgery · UM-MIND · Baltimore, MD · Full-time

Now hiring

You will lead a project on brain fluid transport — glymphatic and cervical lymphatic flow, cerebral oedema after traumatic brain injury, or the effect of airborne particulates on sleep and clearance. Expect to combine in vivo imaging, survival surgery, molecular biology and quantitative analysis, with real ownership of your questions and support to build an independent research profile.

What you’ll do

  • Design and run in vivo studies of CSF and lymphatic dynamics using two-photon and mini-2P imaging and CSF tracer kinetics.
  • Perform survival microsurgery and cranial-window preparation in rodent models of injury and exposure.
  • Analyse data rigorously — immunohistochemistry, particle-tracking velocimetry, behaviour — and present at meetings.
  • Write first-author manuscripts and contribute to grant applications.

What we’re looking for

  • A PhD (or MD/PhD) in neuroscience, physiology, biomedical engineering or a related field — recent or near completion.
  • Hands-on experience with rodent surgery, microscopy or image analysis is strongly preferred.
  • A track record of peer-reviewed publication and clear written and spoken English.
  • Independence, curiosity and the collegiality to thrive in a small, collaborative team.

Why here. You’ll join an NIH- and DoD-funded programme with cutting-edge imaging, close mentoring toward independence, and collaborations with partners at Harvard/MGH, INSERM–Paris-Saclay and the Mayo Clinic — on a campus built around translational neuroscience.

How to apply

Applying is straightforward

  1. Email Dr. Hussain a brief note describing your research interests and why this lab fits your goals.
  2. Attach your CV, a short statement of research experience, and contact details for two or three references.
  3. If there is a formal listing on the UMB careers portal, submit there as well so your application is on file with the institution.
  4. We’ll follow up to arrange a conversation and, for strong matches, a visit to the lab.
