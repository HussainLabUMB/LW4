Studying brain clearance after injury.

We study the glymphatic system — the brain’s waste-clearance network — and how its failure drives swelling, neuroinflammation, and dementia after traumatic brain injury and exposure to harmful pollutants in the environment. Our goal is to turn that pathway into a treatment.

Principal Investigator

Rashad Hussain, PhD

Associate Professor of Neurosurgery

Medicine Institute for Neuroscience Discovery (UM-MIND)
University of Maryland School of Medicine

Dr. Hussain’s work sits at the junction of fundamental and translational neuroscience: glial biology, white matter pathology, traumatic brain injury, sleep regulation, and dysfunction of the glymphatic system. Early in his career he established that stimulating neural androgen receptors can drive myelin repair in chronic demyelination — work published in Brain and protected by two patents on myelin regeneration.

Research efforts were focused on uncovering the question: what happens to the brain’s fluid transport when it is injured, and can restoring that flow change outcomes? The answer, reported in Nature in 2023, was that the noradrenaline surge following trauma shuts down glymphatic and cervical lymphatic drainage — and that blocking adrenergic signalling reopens it, resolving oedema and improving recovery.

Research programme

Four questions we are trying to answer

The lab combines in vivo two-photon and mini-2P imaging, CSF tracer dynamics, survival surgery models, molecular analysis and computational fluid modelling to follow brain fluid from the moment it enters the perivascular space to the moment it leaves the neck.

Glymphatic & lymphatic dynamics

How cerebrospinal fluid moves along perivascular spaces, exchanges with the interstitium through astrocytic aquaporin-4, and drains to cervical lymph nodes — in health, in sleep, and when that flow fails.

Read more

Traumatic brain injury & oedema

Trauma triggers a systemic noradrenaline storm that collapses lymphatic contractility and traps fluid in the brain. We map that cascade and test pan-adrenergic blockade as a way to reverse it.

Read more

Air pollution, burn pits & dementia

Ultrafine particulates reach the brain. We are testing whether they cause Alzheimer’s-related pathology by disrupting sleep and glymphatic clearance — funded by NIH and the DoD Toxic Exposures Research Program.

Glial biology & remyelination

Oligodendrocyte development, integrin-linked kinase signalling, and the neural androgen receptor as a therapeutic target for myelin repair in chronic demyelinating disease.

Featured discovery · Nature, 2023

Improving clearance reverses post-traumatic brain swelling

“Targeting the noradrenergic control of central glymphatic flow may offer a therapeutic approach for treating acute TBI.”

Cerebral oedema raises the risk of death after traumatic brain injury roughly tenfold. We found that the swelling is not simply mechanical: it follows an excessive systemic release of noradrenaline that suppresses both glymphatic and lymphatic flow and reduces the contractility of the cervical lymphatic vessels that return that fluid to the circulation.

Inhibiting α1-, α2- and β-adrenergic receptors after injury normalised central venous pressure, partly restored flow, substantially reduced oedema and improved functional outcomes in mice. It also boosted lymphatic export of cellular debris from the lesion, cutting secondary inflammation and the accumulation of phosphorylated tau.

The team

A small lab that asks large questions

The lab brings together postdoctoral fellows, graduate students, research staff and undergraduates from neuroscience, physiology and engineering backgrounds. Members learn survival microsurgery, cranial-window preparation, two-photon and mini-2P imaging, CSF tracer kinetics, particle-tracking velocimetry, immunohistochemistry and behavioural assessment — often all on the same project.

We collaborate widely, including with partners at Harvard/MGH, INSERM–Paris Saclay and the Mayo Clinic, and with environmental-health and vascular-biology groups whose methods we need and do not have.
