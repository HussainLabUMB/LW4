Home/Research

Following brain fluid from vessel to lymph node

The lab studies how the brain clears its own waste — and what happens to that machinery after injury, poor sleep and environmental exposure. We work across four connected programmes, from perivascular fluid physics to the molecular biology of myelin.

01
Fluid dynamics

Glymphatic & lymphatic dynamics

Cerebrospinal fluid is driven into the brain along peri-arterial spaces, exchanges with the interstitium across astrocytic aquaporin-4 endfeet, and leaves along peri-venous routes, carrying tau, amyloid and cellular debris toward meningeal and cervical lymphatic vessels. We measure this transport directly — in health, during sleep, and when it fails — combining in vivo two-photon and mini-2P imaging, CSF tracer kinetics, particle-tracking velocimetry and computational fluid modelling.

  • Perivascular flow
  • Cervical lymphatics

02
Neurotrauma

Traumatic brain injury & cerebral oedema

Cerebral oedema raises the risk of death after traumatic brain injury roughly tenfold. Our 2023 Nature study showed the swelling is not simply mechanical: trauma triggers a systemic surge of noradrenaline that suppresses glymphatic and lymphatic flow and reduces the contractility of the cervical lymphatic vessels returning that fluid to circulation. Blocking α1-, α2- and β-adrenergic receptors after injury reopened the drain, resolved oedema and improved recovery in mice. We are now mapping that cascade and testing pan-adrenergic blockade as a translational therapy.

  • Noradrenaline
  • Cerebral oedema
  • Adrenergic blockade
  • Phospho-tau
03
Environmental neurotoxicology

Air pollution, burn pits & dementia

Ultrafine particulates from air pollution and military burn pits can reach the brain. We are testing whether they drive Alzheimer’s-related pathology by disrupting sleep and glymphatic clearance — the two processes that normally flush metabolic waste from the brain at night. This work is supported by an NIH R01 and the U.S. Department of Defense Toxic Exposures Research Program.

  • Particulate matter
  • Burn-pit exposure
  • Alzheimer’s pathology
  • ADRD
04
Glial biology

Glial biology & remyelination

Earlier work established that stimulating the neural androgen receptor can drive myelin repair in chronic demyelination — published in Brain and protected by two patents on myelin regeneration. The lab continues to study oligodendrocyte development and integrin-linked kinase signalling, keeping a therapeutic eye on the androgen receptor as a target for restoring white matter after injury and disease.

  • Oligodendrocytes
  • Androgen receptor
  • Integrin-linked kinase
  • Myelin repair
Featured discovery · Nature, 2023

Reopening the drain reverses post-traumatic brain swelling

“Targeting the noradrenergic control of central glymphatic flow may offer a therapeutic approach for treating acute TBI.”

Inhibiting α1-, α2- and β-adrenergic receptors after injury normalised central venous pressure, partly restored glymphatic and lymphatic flow, substantially reduced oedema and improved functional outcomes in mice — while boosting lymphatic export of cellular debris and cutting secondary inflammation and phospho-tau accumulation.
